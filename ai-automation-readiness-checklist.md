# AI Automation Readiness Checklist

### A 50-Point Framework for Evaluating Whether Your Business Is Ready for AI & Automation

Many businesses start AI automation by choosing tools.

A better starting point is understanding:

- What should actually be automated?
- Where can AI create measurable value?
- Is the underlying process ready?
- Is the required data available?
- Can existing systems communicate with each other?
- Where should humans remain involved?
- How will success be measured?

This checklist provides a practical **50-point assessment** for evaluating AI and automation readiness across business processes, data, CRM, integrations, customer communication, governance and measurement.

# How to Use This Checklist

Review each statement and give yourself:

- **1 point** if the statement is substantially true
- **0 points** if it is not yet true or you are unsure

There are **50 checks**, giving a maximum score of **50**.

At the end, calculate your total score to determine your AI Automation Readiness Level.

# 1. Business Process Readiness

Automation works best when the underlying process is understood.

- [ ] **1.** We can identify repetitive manual tasks that consume significant team time.
- [ ] **2.** Our important business processes have clearly defined steps.
- [ ] **3.** We know which processes frequently experience delays or bottlenecks.
- [ ] **4.** We know where human approval or judgment must remain part of the process.
- [ ] **5.** We have identified at least three workflows that could potentially benefit from automation.

### Score: ___ / 5

Examples may include:

`Lead Assignment` · `Follow-ups` · `Appointment Reminders` · `Data Entry` · `Reporting` · `Customer FAQs`

# 2. Data Readiness

AI and automation are only as useful as the information available to them.

- [ ] **6.** Important customer and operational data is stored digitally.
- [ ] **7.** We know which systems contain our important business data.
- [ ] **8.** Customer records are reasonably accurate and structured.
- [ ] **9.** Important fields such as lead source, status, service interest or customer type are captured consistently.
- [ ] **10.** We have a process for correcting, updating or removing inaccurate information.

### Score: ___ / 5

Before introducing AI into a workflow, ask:

> **Does the system have reliable information on which to act?**

Automating bad data usually creates faster bad decisions.

---

# 3. CRM & Lead Management Readiness

For businesses generating enquiries, CRM readiness is often the foundation of revenue automation.

- [ ] **11.** New enquiries are captured in a central CRM or lead-management system.
- [ ] **12.** Leads have clearly defined pipeline stages.
- [ ] **13.** Lead ownership or assignment is visible.
- [ ] **14.** Lead sources are tracked.
- [ ] **15.** Sales activities and outcomes can be reviewed centrally.

### Score: ___ / 5

A basic connected lead journey might look like:

**Lead Source → CRM → Qualification → Assignment → Follow-up → Appointment → Opportunity → Sale**

If leads still live primarily across spreadsheets, personal phones, inboxes and disconnected applications, fixing the foundation may create more value than adding AI immediately.

---

# 4. System Integration Readiness

Automation becomes significantly more useful when systems can exchange information.

- [ ] **16.** We know which tools are involved in our customer journey.
- [ ] **17.** Our important platforms provide APIs, webhooks or supported integrations where needed.
- [ ] **18.** Customer information does not need to be manually copied between every major system.
- [ ] **19.** We understand which system should be the primary source of truth for important records.
- [ ] **20.** We can identify where disconnected systems currently cause delays, duplicate work or lost information.

### Score: ___ / 5

Common integration points include:

**Website ↔ CRM**

**Advertising ↔ CRM**

**CRM ↔ WhatsApp**

**CRM ↔ Email**

**CRM ↔ Calendar**

**CRM ↔ Payment System**

**CRM ↔ Reporting Dashboard**

The goal is not to connect every application.

The goal is to create a reliable flow of information across the systems that matter.

# 5. Lead & Customer Communication Readiness

Many businesses lose opportunities because the enquiry exists, but the response process is inconsistent.

- [ ] **21.** New enquiries receive a timely acknowledgement.
- [ ] **22.** Follow-up does not depend entirely on someone remembering to do it manually.
- [ ] **23.** Customers can receive reminders for important appointments or actions.
- [ ] **24.** Communication history can be reviewed by the appropriate team members.
- [ ] **25.** We have defined when automated communication should stop and a human should take over.

### Score: ___ / 5

Potential automation opportunities include:

- New enquiry acknowledgement
- Sales follow-up
- Appointment reminders
- Missed appointment follow-up
- Quote follow-up
- Payment reminders
- Lead nurturing
- Re-engagement
- Review requests

Automation should improve responsiveness without turning customer communication into spam.

# 6. AI Opportunity Readiness

Not every automation requires AI.

Use AI when interpretation, generation, classification or prioritization adds meaningful value.

- [ ] **26.** We can identify workflows where AI could reduce repetitive interpretation or writing.
- [ ] **27.** We have customer questions or enquiries that could be categorized automatically.
- [ ] **28.** We have opportunities to use AI for lead qualification, prioritization or summarization.
- [ ] **29.** We understand that deterministic rules may be more appropriate than AI for some workflows.
- [ ] **30.** We have at least one AI use case tied to a specific business outcome rather than simply experimenting with AI.

### Score: ___ / 5

Useful AI applications may include:

**AI Lead Qualification**

Classify enquiries based on intent, requirements or fit.

**AI Conversation Summaries**

Summarize conversations before human follow-up.

**AI Response Assistance**

Generate contextual drafts for repetitive enquiries.

**AI Lead Prioritization**

Help identify opportunities that may deserve faster attention.

**AI Data Extraction**

Convert unstructured messages or documents into structured information.

**AI Knowledge Assistance**

Help teams retrieve information from approved internal knowledge sources.

# 7. Human Handoff Readiness

Good automation should know when to stop.

- [ ] **31.** We have defined situations where a human must take over.
- [ ] **32.** Customers have a practical route to reach a person when appropriate.
- [ ] **33.** High-value or sensitive conversations are not left entirely to automation.
- [ ] **34.** Staff can see enough context when an automated interaction is handed over.
- [ ] **35.** Failed or uncertain automated actions can be escalated for human review.

### Score: ___ / 5

A useful operating principle is:

**Automation handles repetition.**

**AI assists with interpretation and prioritization.**

**Humans handle judgment, trust and important decisions.**

---

# 8. Governance, Privacy & Security Readiness

AI automation can move customer and business information across multiple systems.

That requires controls.

- [ ] **36.** We know what customer or company data each automated workflow accesses.
- [ ] **37.** Access to important systems is limited based on appropriate roles.
- [ ] **38.** We review privacy, security and contractual requirements before sending sensitive information to third-party AI services.
- [ ] **39.** Important automated actions can be monitored or audited.
- [ ] **40.** We have a process for disabling or correcting an automation when it behaves unexpectedly.

### Score: ___ / 5

Before deploying an AI workflow, document:

**Data In → Processing → Systems Used → Data Out → Action Taken → Human Oversight**

AI convenience should not come at the cost of uncontrolled access to business or customer information.

---

# 9. Measurement & ROI Readiness

An automation is not successful simply because it runs.

It should improve something measurable.

- [ ] **41.** We have defined the problem the automation is expected to solve.
- [ ] **42.** We know the current baseline before implementing the automation.
- [ ] **43.** We can measure at least one relevant operational or revenue metric.
- [ ] **44.** We can identify the approximate cost of implementing and operating the automation.
- [ ] **45.** We have a process for reviewing whether the automation is producing useful results.

### Score: ___ / 5

Possible metrics include:

- Lead response time
- Follow-up completion rate
- Appointment booking rate
- No-show rate
- Lead-to-opportunity conversion
- Opportunity-to-sale conversion
- Manual hours saved
- Cost per qualified opportunity
- Customer response time
- Revenue influenced by the workflow

The question should not be:

> **How much automation have we implemented?**

The better question is:

> **What business outcome improved because of it?**

---

# 10. Implementation Readiness

Successful automation requires ownership, testing and continuous improvement.

- [ ] **46.** Every important automation has or will have a responsible owner.
- [ ] **47.** New workflows can be tested before being deployed widely.
- [ ] **48.** The team understands how the automation affects their responsibilities.
- [ ] **49.** We can monitor errors, exceptions and unexpected behavior.
- [ ] **50.** We are prepared to improve workflows after reviewing real-world performance.

### Score: ___ / 5

Avoid trying to automate the entire organization at once.

A better implementation sequence is:

**Identify → Prioritize → Build → Test → Measure → Improve → Scale**

# Your AI Automation Maturity Level

## 🔴 0–15 — Not Ready

Your business may benefit more from improving its operational foundations before introducing significant AI automation.

### Prioritize:

- Documenting business processes
- Centralizing customer information
- Establishing CRM discipline
- Improving data quality
- Defining responsibilities
- Identifying repetitive workflows

**Recommended approach:** Build the foundation first.

---

## 🟠 16–25 — Foundation Ready

Some of the necessary infrastructure exists, but disconnected systems or inconsistent processes may limit automation.

### Prioritize:

- CRM adoption
- Data structure
- Lead-source tracking
- Pipeline definition
- Basic integrations
- Communication workflows
- Measurement baselines

**Recommended approach:** Start with simple, rule-based automation before introducing complex AI.

---

## 🟡 26–39 — Automation Ready

Your business has enough structure to automate several meaningful workflows.

### Prioritize:

- Lead routing
- Follow-up automation
- Appointment workflows
- CRM integrations
- Pipeline automation
- Reporting
- Selected AI-assisted processes

**Recommended approach:** Build high-impact workflows and measure their effect before scaling.

---

## 🟢 40–50 — AI Automation Ready

Your organization has many of the foundations required for more advanced AI-assisted workflows.

Potential opportunities include:

- AI lead qualification
- AI conversation analysis
- AI-assisted customer responses
- AI knowledge assistants
- Intelligent lead prioritization
- Automated workflow orchestration
- AI-assisted reporting
- Cross-system revenue automation

**Recommended approach:** Prioritize AI use cases based on measurable business impact, risk and implementation complexity.

# Automation Opportunity Matrix

Once readiness has been assessed, prioritize possible automations using two dimensions:

| Opportunity | Business Impact | Complexity | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| New lead acknowledgement | High | Low | Start Early |
| Lead routing | High | Low–Medium | Start Early |
| Appointment reminders | Medium–High | Low | Start Early |
| Missed lead follow-up | High | Medium | High |
| CRM pipeline automation | High | Medium | High |
| AI lead qualification | High | Medium | High |
| AI conversation summaries | Medium | Medium | Evaluate |
| AI customer assistant | High | Medium–High | Evaluate Carefully |
| Advanced AI agents | Variable | High | Later Stage |

The best first automation is often **not the most sophisticated one**.

Look for:

**High Impact + Low/Medium Complexity + Measurable Outcome**
